# 🧑‍🏫 For Parents & Teachers

A short note about this course. Three minutes.

---

## 🎯 What it covers

The **Past** section, **Units 10–14**, of Abigail's grammar course:

| Unit | Target language |
|---|---|
| 10 | *was / were* (past of *be*) |
| 11 | past simple — regular *-ed* + irregulars (*worked / got / went*) |
| 12 | **past simple negatives & questions** — *I didn't … Did you …?* |
| 13 | past continuous — *I was doing* |
| 14 | past continuous + past simple together — *I was doing … when I did …* |

**Unit 12 receives deliberate extra treatment** (its own step, its own cheat-sheet
page, the longest quiz) because it is where most learners lose marks — usually by
carrying the past into the verb after *did*: ❌ *Did you went*.

---

## 🧠 The three errors this course is designed to prevent

1. **"Did you went?"** → the main verb after *did/didn't* must be the **base form**
2. **"Did you were tired?"** → *was/were* never combine with *did* (→ *Were you tired?*)
3. **"I was play football."** → past continuous always needs **was/were + -ing**

Each is explicitly named and drilled in the course, then tested in the quizzes.

---

## 🗣️ How to use it at home

1. **One unit per sitting** (5 minutes each) — or all five in one 25-minute run.
2. Use the **on-page timer** so she can see the pace.
3. Do the **three-sentence drill** out loud for any verb: *"I went. I didn't go. Did
   you go?"* — this is the single highest-value 30 seconds of practice.
4. Print **⭐ The *did* Rules** and **📖 Irregular Verbs** and put them where she
   does homework.
5. When she gets a quiz question wrong, the feedback explains **why** — read it
   together rather than just re-answering.

---

## 📏 Realistic expectations

- *was/were* and regular *-ed* land quickly (usually within a week).
- **Irregular verbs take real repetition** — 5 per day, spoken aloud, beats a long
  list copied out once.
- Unit 12's rule is *understood* immediately but needs **spaced practice** to
  become automatic; re-run that step's quiz a few days later.
- Unit 14 (the two tenses together) is the hardest — expect it to need a second
  pass, and be reassured that it's normally introduced exactly like this, after the
  two tenses are solid separately.
