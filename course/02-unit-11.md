## 🧠 The spelling rules for **-ed** (3 little rules)

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Most verbs: just add **-ed** | walk → walk**ed** |
| End in **e**: add just **-d** | live → live**d** |
| Short verb ending **consonant + vowel + consonant**: **double** it | stop → sto**pp**ed · plan → pla**nn**ed |
| End in **consonant + y**: change y → **ied** | study → stud**ied** · carry → carr**ied** |

*(But: play → play**ed**, because a vowel comes before the y! 😉)*

---

## 🧠 When do we use the past simple?

When the action is **finished** — often with these words:

| Time words | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **yesterday** | ⬅️ one day back |
| **last** night / week / year | ⬅️ |
| **two days ago** | ⬅️ |
| **in 2020** | ⬅️ |
| **when I was little** | ⬅️ |

- *I **watched** a film **last night**.* 🎬
- *We **went** to the beach **yesterday**.* 🏖️
- *She **ate** noodles **two hours ago**.* 🍜

💡 If you see **yesterday / last / ago**, your verb is almost certainly a past
simple one!

---

## 🧠 Telling a story in the past

Past simple is the **story-telling tense**:

> *Yesterday we **went** to the beach. I **swam** in the sea and my brother
> **built** a sandcastle. Then we **ate** ice cream and we **went** home.*

Every verb is a **finished step** in the story. 🪜

---

## ⚠️ The mistake to avoid

❌ *I **go** to the beach yesterday.* → ✅ *I **went** to the beach yesterday.*

❌ *She **plays** football last Saturday.* → ✅ *She **played** football last Saturday.*

**If the time word says past (*yesterday, last…, ago*), the verb must be past!**
